What the Research Pointed To

We audited the tools teams already use before designing anything new. Each solves part of the problem — understanding which part let us design around the gap, not into it.

01 Messaging Platforms

Teams, Slack

Why it doesn't solve it

"The decision is in the thread — but which thread?"

Built around conversation as the primary artifact. A decision buried inside a 40-message thread is technically present and practically inaccessible. You can search for keywords; you can't search for structured reasoning.

Friction at capture
02 Documentation Platforms

Confluence, Notion

Why it doesn't solve it

"I'll write this up properly — right after this next meeting."

Right format, wrong timing. Switching tools, finding the right page, writing from scratch — all of this happens after the decision, when context has passed. Works for disciplined teams. Doesn't work for most teams under real workload.

03 Video Tools

Loom

Why it doesn't solve it

"The context is in the recording — but you can't search a recording."

Excellent for explaining complexity with tone and context. But a video is a communication format — not a retrieval system. You can't query a library of Looms for a specific piece of reasoning six months later.

04 Task Management

Jira, Asana

Why it doesn't solve it

"Ticket closed. No record of why it was scoped this way."

Designed to track what is being done and whether it's done. The reasoning behind how the work was scoped — why one direction was chosen over another — is absent by design.

The gap No existing tool captures a decision as a structured artifact at the moment it happens and makes it retrievable from a central place later.
