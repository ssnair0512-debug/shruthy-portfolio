Decisions happen across many surfaces at once — a message in chat, a comment in a design file, an email chain, a note from a meeting. Each captures something. None of them make context findable later.

The people who pay the cost are the ones who come in after: new joiners piecing together why things are the way they are, mid-project inheritors trying to make good decisions without the full picture, and managers constantly pulled in to provide context that was never written down.

They search. They find fragments. They make assumptions. Sometimes those assumptions lead to work being undone with no clear understanding of why.

The root cause

A missing record of reasoning is invisible. Because it is invisible, it rarely gets identified or fixed.

Who pays the cost

Not the person who made the decision — the people who come in after and have to reconstruct context that was never written down.
