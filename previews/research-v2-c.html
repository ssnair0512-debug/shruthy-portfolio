Research

What the Research Pointed To

We audited the tools teams already use before designing anything new. Each solves part of the problem — understanding which part let us design around the gap, not into it.

01 Messaging Platforms Teams, Slack Context buried
↑ The actual decision is in the highlighted message — buried inside message 23 of a 40-message thread. Technically present. Practically inaccessible.

These platforms are built around conversation as the primary artifact, not decisions. You can search for keywords, but not for structured reasoning. Context exists — it just can't be found when it matters.

02 Documentation Platforms Confluence, Notion Friction at capture
+ New page
↑ Creating a new page requires switching context, finding the right location, choosing a format, and writing from scratch — all after the decision has already been made.

Right format, wrong timing. The motivation to document collapses the moment the decision is made and everyone moves on. This works for disciplined, well-resourced teams — not most teams under real workload.

03 Video Tools Loom Not queryable
4:23 / 12:07
↑ The reasoning is somewhere in this 12-minute recording. But you can't search "why did we drop the sidebar nav" across a folder of videos.

Excellent for explaining complexity with tone and context. But a video is a communication format — not a retrieval system. You can't query a library of Looms for a specific piece of reasoning six months later.

04 Task Management Jira, Asana What, never why
Status: Done · Reason: —
↑ Status: Done. Reason: —. The ticket tracks that the work was completed. There is no field for why it was scoped this way instead of another way.

Designed to track what is being done and whether it's done. The reasoning behind how the work was scoped — why one direction was chosen over another — is absent by design.

The gap No existing tool captures a decision as a structured artifact at the moment it happens and makes it retrievable from a central place later.
